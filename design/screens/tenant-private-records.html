Our own records card (/private-records, /private-records/[proposalId]; OWN-03, OWN-04, OWN-05, INV-07, PRO-03). The page is for holders of private records approve, which Compliance officer and Approver carry and no platform role or key scope does; everyone else gets state 13. It lists the bank's own proposals, those its own agent filed through runner events for an approved scope item (admin-footprint.html, states 21 to 29) and those a colleague filed, under Waiting, Approved and Rejected, newest first. A row's pills are, in order, the proposal kind (notice), the status (Waiting warning, Approved positive, Rejected information) and, when the proposer's kind is agent, "Proposed by our agent" (brand, the tone of an agent version); a person proposer is meta text. Then the title, and as meta the scope item it answers, the agent and its version or the person, the time and the source's host. The detail puts the AI-drafted callout above the fields (sand, "Drafted by our agent" as a microlabel in brass, naming the agent, its version, the run and the model the run reported) and each field beside the numbered source it came from, since the door takes no field without one. The callout stays until a person decides, so AI output is labelled until it is confirmed. There are no corrections before approving: the reviewer approves what the agent drafted or rejects it with a reason, and the agent may file again. Approve opens the dialog in state 6 and then the passkey step-up (auth-step-up.html, the server's 403 step_up_required retries once), and the proposer, person or agent, never approves: a person sees state 9 and the server answers 409 four_eyes_violation. An agent never approves here either, since no key reaches these routes. Reject needs a reason and no step-up, because it applies nothing. A decision that came second renders state 10. Another bank's proposal, a platform session and a support session all answer 404 and render state 14, which is the shared not-found state and never says the record exists. A control (OWN-05) is a linked internal item of the control kind (REG-05, D-99) and says which of our obligations it serves. Two buttons share a row on a phone, the primary on the right.

"Private to us" (state 15; OWN-04, INV-S15, J-12). A record whose owner kind is the tenant wears the marker as the first pill of its inventory row, its obligation head and its instrument head, as an outlined information pill: outlined because that is what the bank's own things wear (tenant tags), never brand, which means the shared library. The own instrument's short name follows outlined for the same reason. The register's applicability and compliance pills follow as on any obligation (D-75), because the register decides an own record exactly as a shared one. The provenance line names the proposing agent and its version, the source host and the person who approved it, so the record reads machine-proposed and verified by a person, never machine-confirmed. The marker says only who sees the record; it is not a link and not a filter. When the shared library later covers the same regulation, both rows show side by side and nothing links or merges them. The marker is the one sentence this card adds to the inventory, obligation and instrument cards; those cards keep their own slot order after it.
