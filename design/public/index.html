§ 1Why the spreadsheet fails

A spreadsheet cannot testify.

Most banks keep their obligations in a spreadsheet and their regulatory watch in an inbox. Both hold until someone asks a question neither can answer.

What changed in this obligation since last year. Who decided it applied to us, and on what evidence. What we were told and did not act on. The spreadsheet has one cell and one colour; the inbox has arrivals and no proof of what never arrived.

Compliance Watch answers those questions by construction. Nothing is overwritten, every version carries its effective date, and every write lands in the audit trail in the same transaction as the change itself.

  • No version, no diff

    A cell is edited and the previous wording is gone, along with the date it stopped being true.

  • A status, not a decision

    Green says compliant. Nothing says who decided, when, under which passkey, or what they read first.

  • Arrivals, not coverage

    An inbox proves what reached you. A vendor review asks what did not, and why.

§ 2Four things, no more

One inventory, one feed, one record.

Inventory

Every obligation, with its history

One record per obligation, in five languages, with versions, effective dates and a readable diff between any two. Stable keys that never change, so a reference made three years ago still resolves.

Watch

Agents find it, people decide it

Research agents read the sources every day and file proposals. A proposal is the only door into the shared library: no agent writes to it directly, and no API key holds a scope that reaches it.

Ask

A question, answered from the provision

Hybrid search across the library and your own zone, answered with citations to the provision and the version it came from. First words on screen inside two seconds, streamed.

Evidence

What applies, where you stand

Applicability and compliance status per legal entity, gaps with an owner and a date, cases that carry the work, and sign-off by a second person with a passkey.

§ 3From sighting to sign-off

Every change takes the same seven steps.

The steps are typed, and so is the actor at each one. Nothing skips a step, and the record of the step is written before anyone can act on the next.

  1. Sighted. A source publishes; the agent reads it the same day and classifies it.agent
  2. Proposed. The agent drafts the change against the current version and files it, labelled as drafted.agent
  3. Reviewed. A platform editor reads the proposal beside the source it came from.platform editor
  4. Approved. A second, independent principal approves with a passkey, never the one who proposed.second principal
  5. Applied. A new version enters the shared library with its effective date. The old one stays readable.library
  6. Assessed. Your bank decides whether it applies, to which entity, and where you stand today.your bank
  7. Signed off. A second person in your bank signs with a passkey, and the evidence is closed.second person

Four eyes at every gate, enforced by a check constraint in the database rather than by convention. AI output stays labelled until a person confirms it, and a record confirmed by an agent says so in words, never that a person verified it.

§ 4What is shared, what is yours

The library is shared. The judgement is yours.

The shared library

Sourced public facts: instruments, provisions, obligations, authorities, effective dates and the citations behind them. Identical for every bank, so a correction reaches all of them at once.

Your zone

Applicability, compliance status per legal entity, gaps, cases, evidence and sign-off. Under forced row-level security: the application role cannot bypass it, and the application refuses to start on a role that can.

Your judgement never reaches another bank, and no tenant content reaches a log, an error report or a model endpoint that has not been approved.

§ 6What a vendor review asks

Built to be examined.

AccessPasskeys only. No passwords, ever. The emailed enrolment code works once and stops working the moment your first passkey exists. No self-service reset: your administrator re-enrols you.
Four eyesA check constraint, not a policy. Approvals, sign-off, footprint changes, exports, key creation and role changes each need a second principal and a passkey at the moment of the act.
IsolationEvery tenant row carries its tenant and sits under enabled and forced row-level security. The application role holds no bypass, and the application will not boot on a role that does.
Model useApproved endpoints in the EU. Every model call is logged with its prompt shape, its version and its cost. Fetched content is treated as untrusted, never as instruction.
The ledgerNothing is overwritten. Versions with effective dates, soft-deleted evidence, append-only ledgers held by a database trigger, and an audit row written in the same transaction as the change.
RetentionTen years after a record was last used, a closed case or a removed piece of evidence is deleted whole by the daily purge. The cutoff can never be set younger than a year.
LeavingTwo people approve the exit with a passkey, the final export is taken, and then every row of yours is deleted, the audit trail included.
The packAn assurance pack your vendor review can read: the architecture, the control list, the audit model, the sub-processors and the tests that hold each control in place.
§ 7Before you ask

Questions we are asked.

Does the AI decide anything?

No. It finds, drafts and proposes. Its output carries a label until a person confirms it, and a record an agent confirmed says so, naming both agents. No agent writes to the library and no model call goes unlogged.

Can we start with one legal entity?

Yes. The footprint is set by your administrator and changed only by two people with a passkey, so adding an entity later is a decision with a record, not a settings change.

Who else sees our assessments?

Nobody. Your judgement, your cases and your evidence live in your own zone. What is shared is the sourced public fact, which is the same for every bank.

What if the library is wrong?

You report the problem from the record. It lands in the platform queue with your note beside the source, and the correction reaches every bank at once, with its own version.

How do people get in?

Your administrator invites them. They enrol one passkey, then a second, and the emailed code stops working. There is no password to phish and no reset link to intercept.

What does it cost?

Pricing on application, by the size of the footprint in scope: legal entities, licences and jurisdictions. No per-seat charge for the people who only read.

Access is by invitation.

Tell us where you work and we will arrange an introduction. Your administrator enrols you, you register a passkey, and then a second one for the day you lose the first.

We reply from a named person, not a queue.
