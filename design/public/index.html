demonoun

  1. The real screens of Compliance Watch, opened with a made-up bank inside them.
  2. The quickest way to judge the product, because you can read a briefing, open an obligation and sign a change off before you talk to anyone.

spreadsheetsee inventorynoun, obsolete

  1. A table of obligations in which the earlier wording of an edited cell is gone stays readable, with the dates it applied.
  2. A place where a status of compliant is one word in a cell carries a name, the evidence and a second signature.

inboxsee watchnoun, obsolete

  1. A view of regulatory news that holds whatever reached you what a fixed list of sources published, checked on a schedule.

watchverb

  1. To check each regulator and law gazette on a schedule, in the language it publishes in.
  2. To file what was found as a proposal, which a second agent with its own key confirms against the source before the library changes. Watch sighted the amendment at the six o'clock check and filed it as a proposal.

See also source, sign off.

inventorynoun

  1. Every obligation your bank holds, where each one keeps all its versions and the dates they took effect.
  2. A record that shows a change sentence by sentence against the wording it replaced, so the earlier text stays readable.

askverb

  1. To put a question to the inventory and receive an answer with citations to the provisions it came from.
  2. To be told where a change is pending and where the inventory has a gap, in place of a guess.

evidencenoun

  1. The applicability and compliance status of an obligation, recorded for each legal entity with an owner and a date.
  2. Your bank's own file, private to your zone and kept apart from other banks by row-level security in the database. The reviewer asked for the evidence, and it carried two names.

sign offverb

  1. To close a change with a second signature. It is the last of six steps, and every change takes them in this order.
  1. Sightedby an agent, at the next scheduled check
  2. Proposedby the agent, against the wording in force
  3. Confirmedby a second agent with its own key
  4. Appliedto the library, where the old version stays readable
  5. Assessedby your bank, which decides whether and how it applies
  6. Signed offby a second person in your bank

sourcenoun

  1. A regulator or law gazette that agents check for your bank. The list is part of the record, so you can show a reviewer what was covered.
