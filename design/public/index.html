§ 1Why the spreadsheet fails

A spreadsheet cannot testify.

Most banks keep their obligations in a spreadsheet and their regulatory watch in an inbox.

That works until a reviewer asks what the obligation said two years ago, who decided it reached the Norwegian entity, and what they had read before deciding. Here, nothing is overwritten, so the answers are still there.

  • No version, no difference

    The earlier wording is gone, and so is the date it stopped being true.

  • A colour, not a decision

    Green says compliant. It does not say who decided that, or on what.

  • Arrivals, not coverage

    An inbox is evidence of what reached you, and of nothing else.

§ 2Four things, no more

One inventory, one feed, one record.

Inventory

Every obligation, with its history

Each version, the date it took effect, and a readable difference between any two. A reference made three years ago still resolves to the wording it meant then.

Watch

Agents find it, your bank decides it

Agents read the sources every day and file what they find as proposals. Nothing else reaches the library.

Ask

A question, answered from the provision

The answer quotes the provision it rests on, in the language it was published in, and names its version.

Evidence

What applies, and where you stand

Status for each legal entity, gaps with an owner and a date, and a sign-off by a second person in your bank.

§ 3From sighting to sign-off

Every change takes the same six steps.

  1. Sighted. A regulator publishes, and an agent reads it that day.agent
  2. Proposed. The agent drafts the change against the wording in force.agent
  3. Confirmed. A second agent, built differently, checks the draft against the source. Nothing confirms its own work.second agent
  4. Applied. A new version, with the date it takes effect. The old one stays readable.library
  5. Assessed. Your bank decides whether it reaches you, and where you stand.your bank
  6. Signed off. A second person in your bank signs, and the evidence closes.your bank

A confirmed fact names both agents and never reads as verified by a person. The reading of it is yours, and a person signs that.

§ 4What is shared, what is yours

The library is shared. The judgement is yours.

The shared library

Instruments, provisions, obligations and effective dates, each with its citation. The same for every bank, so one correction reaches all of them.

§ 7Before you ask

Questions we are asked.

Does the AI decide anything?

It finds, drafts and proposes. What a second agent confirms is marked as confirmed by machine; what it means for your bank is yours to decide.

Can we start with one legal entity?

Yes. Widening the footprint later is a decision with a record behind it, not a setting somebody changed on a Friday.

Who else sees our assessments?

Nobody. What is shared is the public fact. What you make of it stays in your zone.

What if the library is wrong?

Say so on the record; your note stays inside your bank. The correction arrives the other way, from the agents' re-check against the source, through the same proposal door as everything else.

How do people get in?

Your administrator invites them, and they enrol on their own device. There is no password to set.

What does it cost?

On application, against the footprint in scope. Reading costs nothing per head: a record nobody can open is worth little.
